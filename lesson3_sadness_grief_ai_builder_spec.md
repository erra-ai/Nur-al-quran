**Program:** Strong Heart, Wise Mind — Quranic Life Skills for Muslim Kids
**Age Group:** 10–13
**Core Islamic Value:** Sadness is human, not shameful — Allah does not ask us to hide heavy hearts; He asks us to bring them to Him and to each other
**Skill:** Name it honestly → Tell a safe person → Turn to Allah → Hold onto hope
**Format:** Standalone HTML (`lesson3.html`, same pattern as lesson1/lesson2, iframe-loaded)

---

## Learning Goal
Teach children that sadness and grief are **not weakness and not a failure of faith**. The heaviest sadness can belong to the strongest hearts — Prophet Yaqub cried until he went blind from grief, and the Prophet ﷺ himself cried for his son, his grandson, his friends, and his ummah. What Islam forbids is only despair (thinking Allah has forgotten you) — not tears.

A Muslim child learns: **"I can be honest: 'I feel sad.' I don't carry it alone — I tell a safe person, I tell Allah, and I remember that with hardship comes ease. My tears can even be worship."**

## Section Structure (same 8-section shape as Lessons 1–2)

### 1. Main Story: Sara and Jaddu's Prayer Mat
- Sara's grandfather (Jaddu) died last week. At the funeral she felt numb. People say "be strong."
- Days later the ache arrives: his prayer mat still folded, his smell of dates and oud, his chair on the balcony
- Her aunt says "Don't cry, that hurts him in his grave" (a wrong cultural message the lesson corrects — dua and tears do not hurt the dead; they help them)
- Harmful thought: "Nobody understands. If I stop crying maybe Mama won't be sad too. I should pretend I'm fine."
- Body signs: heavy chest, tired all the time, no appetite, wanting to be alone
- Mama sits next to her and says: "Tears don't weaken your iman. The Prophet ﷺ cried when his baby son Ibrahim died and said the eyes tear and the heart is sad, but we say only what pleases Allah."
- Sara names it honestly: "I miss him so much it hurts." They cry together, then make dua together
- Sara starts "Project Jaddu": giving charity in his name, teaching his favorite surah to her little brother, keeping his friendships alive
- Ending: the sadness does not vanish; it becomes bearable because she carries it honestly, together with her family, with Allah. Grief shrinks when shared; it ferments when hidden.

### 2. Quran Anchor — Surah Yusuf 12:84 & 12:86 (Yaqub's grief) + Ash-Sharh 94:5–6 (the promise)
12:84: وَتَوَلَّىٰ عَنْهُمْ وَقَالَ يَـٰٓأَسَفَىٰ عَلَىٰ يُوسُفَ وَٱبْيَضَّتْ عَيْنَاهُ مِنَ ٱلْحُزْنِ فَهُوَ كَظِيمٌ
*"And he turned away from them and said, 'Oh, my sorrow over Joseph,' and his eyes became white from grief, for he was [of that] a suppressor."* (ClearQuran — Talal Itani, verified from quran.com API)
12:86: قَالَ إِنَّمَآ أَشْكُوا۟ بَثِّى وَحُزْنِىٓ إِلَى ٱللَّهِ وَأَعْلَمُ مِنَ ٱللَّهِ مَا لَا تَعْلَمُونَ
*"He said, 'I only complain of my suffering and my grief to Allah, and I know from Allah that which you do not know.'"* (verified from quran.com API)
- Yaqub was a PROPHET — one of the strongest believers who ever lived — and he cried from grief for decades. Crying never took away his prophethood. It cannot take away your worth either.
- But WHERE he poured it out matters: "I complain only to Allah" — honesty with Allah, not despair about Allah.
- The promise — Ash-Sharh 94:5–6: فَإِنَّ مَعَ ٱلْعُسْرِ يُسْرًا * إِنَّ مَعَ ٱلْعُسْرِ يُسْرًا "For indeed, with hardship [will be] ease. Indeed, with hardship [will be] ease." — said twice, so it cannot be broken.
- Balance card: Islam does not say "never be sad." It says: be honest, turn to Allah, do not despair — despair says Allah forgot you, and that is the only thought sadness is not allowed to grow into.

### 3. Prophetic Lens (ALL verified)
1. **The Prophet's ﷺ tears:** He took his dying grandson and his eyes filled with tears. Sa'd ibn Ubadah said "Even you, Messenger of Allah?" He said: "إِنَّهَا رَحْمَةٌ — It is mercy. Allah places mercy in the hearts of His servants, and Allah only shows mercy to the merciful ones." (Sahih al-Bukhari 1284)
2. **The grief words:** "The eyes shed tears, the heart grieves, but we do not say except what pleases our Lord." (Sahih al-Bukhari 1303 — the same hadith the child learned in Lesson 1; shown again as the whole-program rule)
3. **The Prophet's ﷺ sadness dua:** «اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي أَعُوذُ بِكَ مِنَ الْهَمِّ وَالْحَزَنِ...» — "O Allah, I seek refuge in You from anxiety and grief, from weakness and laziness, from miserliness and cowardice, from being overwhelmed by debt and overpowered by men." (Sahih al-Bukhari 6369 — the Prophet ﷺ said this regularly; Anas served him and heard it constantly. If the heaviest hearts need this dua, ours do too.)
4. **Even a thorn:** «مَا يُصِيبُ الْمُسْلِمَ مِنْ نَصَبٍ وَلَا وَصَبٍ وَلَا هَمٍّ وَلَا حُزْنٍ وَلَا أَذًى وَلَا غَمٍّ حَتَّى الشَّوْكَةِ يُشَاكُهَا إِلَّا كَفَّرَ اللَّهُ بِهَا مِنْ خَطَايَاهُ» — "No fatigue, illness, worry, sadness, harm, or distress befalls a Muslim — even a thorn that pricks him — but that Allah expiates some of his sins for it." (Sahih al-Bukhari 5641, Sahih Muslim 2573 — verified via hadeethenc.com #3701, متفق عليه). The ache you feel is NOT wasted; Allah is paying you for it.
5. Hope card: "When a person dies, his deeds are cut off except three: ongoing charity (sadaqah jariyah), knowledge benefited from, and a righteous child who prays for him." (Sahih Muslim 1631) — this is the door of "Project Jaddu": you can keep loving someone after their death through good done in their name and dua for them.

### 4. Understand the Difference (Signal / Unhelpful Thought / Wise Choice)
- Signal: "I lost something I loved. My heart is telling me it mattered. I need time, love, and Allah."
- Unhelpful thoughts: "Crying means my iman is weak." / "If I stay sad forever it proves how much I loved them." / "Allah is punishing me." / "Nobody cares, so I'll tell nobody."
- Wise choice: Name the sadness honestly. Tell one safe person. Make the Prophet's ﷺ dua. Turn the love into ongoing good deeds.
- Warning box (do NOT teach): "Don't cry — it hurts the dead." Wrong — tears never hurt anyone; dua, charity and good deeds REACH and HELP the dead, and the Prophet ﷺ himself cried. Also do NOT teach: "Good Muslims are always happy." Yaqub cried for decades and was a prophet.
- Also: name the one forbidden door — despair (thinking Allah has abandoned you). Sadness walks TO Allah; despair walks away from Him. That is the difference.

### 5. Main SEL Tool — Heart First Aid: 4 steps (H.A.T.H adapted to the Islamic frame)
1. **H — Honestly name it (قُلْ بِصِدْق):** Say out loud: "I feel sad. I miss them. My heart hurts." Naming is not weakness — Yaqub said "Oh, my sorrow over Yusuf." The feeling needs a name to be carried.
2. **A — Ask for company (لَا تَحْمِلْ وَحْدَك):** Tell ONE safe person today. Grief shrinks when shared. A heavy heart was never meant to be carried alone — Allah pairs people together for this.
3. **T — Turn to Allah (أَشْكُ إِلَيْهِ لَا عَنْهُ):** Complain TO Allah, not ABOUT your fate away from Him. Say the Prophet's ﷺ dua: اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي أَعُوذُ بِكَ مِنَ الْهَمِّ وَالْحَزَنِ. Put your forehead down — sujood is the place sadness leaves the body.
4. **H — Hold the promise + Hope in action (وَإِنَّ مَعَ ٱلْعُسْرِ يُسْرًا):** Trust the twice-repeated promise of Ash-Sharh. Turn love into ongoing good: give charity in their name, pray for them, do what they loved. "Project good-deed-in-their-name" — sadaqah jariyah (Muslim 1631).
Rule card: "Sadness is not my enemy and not my shame. I name it honestly, I carry it with company, I bring it to Allah, and I hold onto His twice-given promise."
Interactive **Heart Weight Scale** (4 levels like Lesson 2's anger scale): 1–3 blue days (name + dua + rest), 4–6 heavy (tell your safe person), 7–8 dark (adult NOW + keep telling — hiding is dangerous), 9–10 (you are sick, not bad — a trusted adult + a doctor or counselor is how Allah sends healing; stay with it, healing comes in waves)

### 6. Transfer Practice: Omar the New Kid
- Family moved cities for Baba's job; left his best friend Samir and grandma behind
- Lunch break alone; the old city on his phone makes the ache worse
- Checkpoints: which is the honest naming? ("I feel lonely and sad" — not "I'm fine," not "I hate everyone")
- Which choice follows the 4 steps? (tell Mum + make the dua + call Samir to keep the bond + join the masjid class to grow new roots)
- Ending: he keeps the old friendship ALIVE (calling = maintaining ties) while slowly building new ones. Missing someone is love with nowhere to go yet — make it a project.

### 7. Home Challenge: My Sadness Support Map
- Three lines: (1) My body's sadness warning signs. (2) My one safe person I will tell this week. (3) My dua word: اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي أَعُوذُ بِكَ مِنَ الْهَمِّ وَالْحَزَنِ
- Mission: say the dua once today + do one good deed in the name of someone you miss (or one act of kindness to someone who is sad)

### 8. Parent / Teacher Guide
- Avoid saying: "Stop crying." / "He's in a better place, don't be sad" (too early — correct doctrine, wrong timing; truth spoken before grief is ready feels like being dismissed) / "Big kids don't cry." / "You're embarrassing yourself."
- Say instead: "I can see your heart is heavy." / "Tell me — what do you miss most?" / "The Prophet ﷺ cried too. Your tears are human." / "Let's make dua together — I'll go first."
- Grief in children comes in waves and play — a sad child can laugh 5 minutes later and that is NOT disrespect; it is how children pace grief.
- The one red flag adults must watch: when heaviness blocks sleeping/eating/school for weeks → professional help is tawakkul + action, not weak faith
- Interactive teach-back ordering game: Honestly name → Tell a safe person → Turn to Allah → Hope in action
- Final scenario MCQ (Yasmin's grandmother's house, same pattern as Lesson 2)
