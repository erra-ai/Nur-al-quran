Part 10 · When You Don't Know What Happens Next

Tawakkul and Uncertainty

Tie your camel, then trust Allah — the heart rests where the hands work.

A story-centered Islamic SEL lesson that teaches children how to act bravely when the future is fog: do your honest part, tie the camel, and hand the result to the Only One who owns tomorrow.

Age10–13
Time25–30 min
SEL SkillTrust & decision-making
Main ToolThe Two-Hand Rule: Do — Tie — Ask — Let go

1. Main Story: The Camp Decision

Two choices sat on the kitchen table like a fork in the road: the football camp Baba could barely afford, or the science week the teacher called 'once in a lifetime.' Eleven days to decide. And the fog was thick — what if the camp coach hates me? What if science week is boring and we wasted the money?

Zayd spiralled. He asked seven people what to do, redrew the pros-and-cons list three times, and slept badly for two nights — because the truth nobody said out loud was this: none of the answers could be KNOWN. That is what uncertainty IS. His mother watched him a day, then said: 'Ya habibi, let me show you the two hands.'

'I have to get this PERFECT, because if I choose wrong, my whole year breaks. I must decide it all by myself, right now, with total certainty!'

What did Zayd's loop actually demand of him?

Mama took two sticky notes. HAND ONE — DO what you own: ask the science teacher what the week actually covers; get the camp coach's number and ask about him; write the real budget with Baba. HAND TWO — ASK Allah: consult the wise, pray salat al-istikhara (the prayer of asking Allah for good), and then Mama said the sentence she had learned twice in her life and lived both times: 'The result was never yours.'

Zayd did his part for six days, slept properly for the first time on night two, and chose camp. Half of science week's 'magic' turned out to be videos the teacher could have shown on any Tuesday. The camp was one of the hardest, best weeks of his summer — and his best friend there is now his team captain. Not because he guessed right, but because he DID his part and LET GO of the part he never owned.

'I work with my hands, I tie with my effort, I ask Allah — and I release the future to its Owner. The result was never mine, and I was never alone in it.'

Why did Zayd sleep better on night two?

2. Quran Anchor

The Quran's definition of trust is a sequence of verbs, not a feeling:

فَإِذَا عَزَمْتَ فَتَوَكَّلْ عَلَى ٱللَّهِ ۚ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يُحِبُّ ٱلْمُتَوَكِّلِينَ

“And when you have decided, then rely upon Allah. Indeed, Allah loves those who rely [upon Him].”

Surah Ali 'Imran 3:159 · ClearQuran (Talal Itani)

وَمَن يَتَوَكَّلْ عَلَى ٱللَّهِ فَهُوَ حَسْبُهُۥٓ

“And whoever relies upon Allah — then He is sufficient for him.”

Surah At-Talaq 65:3 · ClearQuran (Talal Itani)

Read 3:159's ORDER: consult → decide → THEN rely. The verse never says 'guess and hope.' And 'hasbuh' in 65:3 means Allah is SUFFICIENT — yet the bird hadith proves the bird still flies at dawn: trust has shoes.

Important balance: tawakkul is NOT 'I don't care, Allah will sort it' — that is laziness wearing a pious coat. The Prophet ﷺ answered the man who wanted to leave his camel untied: 'Tie it, AND trust.' Full effort with a fully free heart — hands working, heart resting.

3. Prophetic Lens

Two prophetic moments that define the whole lesson:

قَالَ رَجُلٌ: يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ, أُرْسِلُهَا وَأَتَوَكَّلُ, أَوْ أَعْقِلُهَا وَأَتَوَكَّلُ? قَالَ: اعْقِلْهَا وَتَوَكَّلْ

A man asked: 'O Messenger of Allah — should I release my camel and trust, or tie it and trust?' He said: "Tie it, and trust."

Jami' at-Tirmidhi 2517 · hasan (graded in Sahih al-Jami' 1068 by al-Albani)

لَوْ أَنَّكُمْ تَتَوَكَّلُونَ عَلَى اللَّهِ حَقَّ تَوَكُّلِهِ لَرَزَقَكُمْ كَمَا يَرْزُقُ الطَّيْرَ, تَغْدُو خِمَاصًا وَتَرُوحُ بِطَانًا

"If you relied upon Allah with true reliance, He would provide for you as He provides for the bird: it leaves hungry in the morning and returns full in the evening."

Jami' at-Tirmidhi 2344 · sahih (verified at hadeethenc.com #4721)

Look at the bird: it does not fly out full — it flies out HUNGRY. Dependence on Allah and daily movement are ONE act in that image. Your uncertain week is the morning flight; the full return is what you cannot yet see.

The fog whispers

"You must control the outcome or you're finished."

Tawakkul answers

"I own the effort, Allah owns the result — and I was never carrying what was never mine."

Zayd's six days of honest work were his camel-rope; istikhara and the released heart were his flying out hungry. Both together — that is the whole religion of decisions.

4. Understand the Difference

Signal

"The future is unseen and my mind is trying to solve it like a math problem. It is not a problem to solve; it is a fog to walk through with a Guide."

Unhelpful Thought or Urge

"Decide with total certainty NOW — or refuse to decide at all — or let ten people decide for me."

Wise Choice

DO your owned part fully → TIE the camel (all the preparation) → ASK Allah (shura + istikhara) → LET GO of the result with hasbunallahu wa ni'mal wakil.

Do not teach: "If you truly had tawakkul you'd never feel anxious about the future" — false. The Prophet ﷺ prepared, consulted, and felt the weight of decisions too. Tawakkul is action PLUS release; anxiety is the brain asking for certainty Allah never gave anyone. Teaching children that uncertainty-feelings equal weak faith creates either panic or fake bravado.

Name istikhara simply: 'O Allah, I ask You for good in this choice and to turn what is harmful away from me.' It is a prayer you pray AFTER gathering facts — not a dream lottery.

5. The Two-Hand Rule

1

Do your part

اِسْعَ

List the facts you own: ask the teacher, make the calls, write the numbers. The visible list kills the invisible monster.

2

Tie the camel

اِعْقِلْهَا

Preparation is part of trust, not its opposite. Every rope you tie is a sentence you say to Allah: 'I take this seriously.'

3

Ask Allah

سْتَخِرْ

Consult the wise (shura). Pray istikhara: ask Allah to pull this choice toward your good and push the harmful away.

4

Release the result

فَوَكِّلْ

After the decision: hasbunallahu wa ni'mal wakil. You are the worker, not the Owner — and sleep belongs to Allah.

Hands busy, heart surrendered. I tie the camel and I trust. The result was never mine.

6. Transfer Practice: The School Trip Form

The trip form due Friday needed a forty-dirham fee, and Omar knew Baba's paycheck was late — again. His instinct: don't ask, just tell the teacher no, and hide that he wanted to go badly. He called that being 'understanding'; really it was grieving in advance to dodge the conversation.

Which move is the Two-Hand Rule?

Baba's answer: 'We ask Allah tonight, together. If it is meant, a way appears.' That night they made du'a. Wednesday: Mama's neighbor offered to lend the amount until payday — simply, warmly, no drama. Omar went on the trip. Sometimes the camel you tied with honesty gets pulled across the river by doors you could never have pushed open alone.

7. My Next Foggy Decision

Pick ONE real decision you are carrying:

  1. My facts list: the three questions I will actually ask this week (a teacher, an adult, the coach).
  2. My istikhara night: schedule it — two rak'ahs and the du'a this week, for THIS decision.
  3. My release line: write 'I own the effort; the result belongs to Allah' — tape it where the fog loops at you.

Run the two hands on your foggy decision before the next lesson, and report where the rope got tied.

Wrong choices are survivable — that is the secret nobody tells children: adults live with decisions that were wrong, and the sky stayed down anyway. Allah is closer to the one who acted honestly, even imperfectly, than to the one who froze perfectly.

8. Parent / Teacher Guide

Avoid saying

  • "Just trust — stop thinking so much."
  • "Whatever happens happens" (shrugging is not tawakkul).
  • "You should have known better" — punishing the learner for fog.

Say instead

  • "What part is yours? What part is Allah's? Put each in its own box."
  • "Tell me what you're afraid of — let's look at it once, fully."
  • "Want to make istikhara together?"
