# Lesson 2 — "Anger: Pause Before Harm" — AI Builder Specification
## Overview
**Title:** Anger: Pause Before Harm
**Program:** Strong Heart, Wise Mind — Quranic Life Skills for Muslim Kids
**Age Group:** 10–13
**Core Islamic Value:** Anger is a real feeling Allah created; the test is what we DO in the pause before harm
**Skill:** Notice → Name → Pause (STOP) → Choose
**Format:** Standalone HTML (same pattern as lesson1.html, loaded in the Life Skills iframe)

---

## Learning Goal
Teach children that anger is **not a sin by itself** — it is a storm that passes through everyone, including the companions of the Prophet ﷺ. The danger is the first few seconds after anger arrives: the moment when a person says words or does actions they regret for the rest of their life.

A Muslim child learns: **"Anger is a feeling, not a command. Between the feeling and the action, there is a pause — and in that pause lives my choice and my faith."**

## Section Structure (same shape as Lesson 1)

### 1. Main Story: Karim's Tower
- Karim spends all afternoon building a block tower with his little sister's help
- His older cousin Ammar (12) bumps the table while running for a ball — tower collapses
- Body signs: hot face, clenched fists, fast heartbeat, tight jaw
- Harmful thought: "He did it on purpose. He always ruins my things."
- Urge: "Push him. Scream at him. Break his game."
- Karim remembers his father taught him the four-step pause skill
- Wise thought: "I feel angry. That does not prove he did it on purpose. I can choose what I do next."
- He says "I need a minute," sits down, breathes, makes wudu intention
- He discovers Ammar did not see the tower — he was chasing the ball
- Ammar apologizes and helps rebuild
- Karim still feels anger, but he protected his words, his hands, and the relationship

### 2. Quran Anchor — Surah Ali 'Imran 3:134
الَّذِينَ يُنْفِقُونَ فِي السَّرَّاءِ وَالضَّرَّاءِ وَالْكَاظِمِينَ الْغَيْظَ وَالْعَافِينَ عَنِ النَّاسِ ۗ وَاللَّهُ يُحِبُّ الْمُحْسِنِينَ
*"Those who spend [in the cause of Allah] during ease and hardship and who restrain anger and who pardon the people — and Allah loves the doers of good."*
(ClearQuran — Talal Itani, verified from quran.com API)
- Three steps in the verse itself: **restrain** (hold the anger back) → **pardon** (let it go) → Allah **loves** you
- Balance card: the verse says "restrain anger," NOT "never feel anger" — the feeling is not the failure; the uncontrolled action is
- Storm weather visual (same grid as Lesson 1): Anger = ⛈️ storm — it arrives, it passes; the house that survives is the one that pauses

### 3. Prophetic Lens — three hadiths (ALL verified against hadeethenc.com / Sahih sources)
1. **The advice:** «لَا تَغْضَبْ» — "Do not become angry." A man said to the Prophet ﷺ: "Advise me." He said: "Do not become angry." The man repeated his request, and the Prophet ﷺ repeated: "Do not become angry." (Sahih al-Bukhari 6116 — hadeethenc.com #4709)
2. **The pause skill:** «إِذَا غَضِبَ أَحَدُكُمْ فَلْيَسْكُتْ» — "When any of you becomes angry, let him keep silent." (Musnad Ahmad, sahih chain) — words are the first weapon anger picks up
3. **The body skill:** «إِذَا غَضِبَ أَحَدُكُمْ وَهُوَ قَائِمٌ فَلْيَجْلِسْ، فَإِنْ ذَهَبَ عَنْهُ الْغَضَبُ وَإِلَّا فَلْيَضْطَجِعْ» — "When one of you becomes angry while standing, let him sit down. If the anger leaves him, [well and good]; otherwise let him lie down." (Sunan Abi Dawud 4782, sahih per al-Albani) — a standing body is ready to strike; a sitting body is not; a lying body cannot
4. Connection card (same grid-2 as Lesson 1): The Prophet ﷺ never struck anyone with his hand in anger (Sahih Muslim 2328). His anger was for Allah's limits being crossed, never for himself. Anger CAN be righteous — when it defends truth and injustice — but even then it obeys rules, it does not explode.

### 4. Understand the Difference (Signal / Unhelpful Thought-Urge / Wise Choice grid)
- Signal: "Something feels unfair. My limits were crossed. I need this to stop."
- Unhelpful thought or urge: "He did it ON PURPOSE. I must hurt him back RIGHT NOW."
- Wise choice: Pause first. Check the thought. Choose words and actions that please Allah.
- Warning box: Do not teach "anger means the person is bad." Anger is human. What makes us good or bad is what we do in the pause.

### 5. Main SEL Tool — The Pause (S.T.O.P. adapted to the Islamic frame)
Four skill cards, same grid as Lesson 1's Notice–Name–Check–Choose, but built around THE PAUSE:
1. **S — Sit (بدّل وضعك):** Change your body position. Standing → sit. Sitting → stand or step away. The Prophet ﷺ taught this with his own words (Abu Dawud 4782).
2. **T — Take a breath + Ta'awwudh (استعذ بالله):** Say أَعُوذُ بِاللَّهِ مِنَ الشَّيْطَانِ الرَّجِيمِ. The Prophet ﷺ said there are words that, if a person says them when angry, the anger leaves him (Sahih al-Bukhari 6115, Sahih Muslim 2610 — story of the two men arguing in the masjid).
3. **O — Only silence (اسكت):** Keep silent until the storm's first wave passes (Musnad Ahmad). Silence protects words you cannot take back.
4. **P — Pour water (توضأ):** Make wudu or wash your face with cool water. Anger is fire, and fire is extinguished with water. (Abu Dawud 4784 — the standing/sitting hadith's companion narration advises wudu)
Rule card: "Anger arrives in seconds. Harm lives for years. In between is the PAUSE — and the pause belongs to me, with Allah's help."

### 6. Transfer Practice: Hana's Group Chat
- Hana sees a message where two classmates joked about her answer in class; she feels the heat rise
- Her fingers move to type something sharp back
- Checkpoints: What is the FIRST pause move? (put the phone down / say ta'awwudh, NOT type faster)
- Which response follows Sit–Ta'awwudh–Silence–Water? (wait, cool down, then ask privately what they meant, or tell a trusted adult if it continues)
- Ending: the joke was a silly misunderstanding; one classmate apologizes; Hana's silence saved a friendship — one typed sentence in anger would have cost it

### 7. Home Challenge: My Anger Pause Plan
- Write your personal pause plan (my body sign / my first pause move / who I can talk to)
- Anger Scale 1–10 with what to do at each level (same interactive pattern as the archived lesson's Sabr Scale): 1–3 notice, 4–6 pause moves start, 7–8 leave the situation + wudu, 9–10 get a trusted adult NOW
- Mission: practice the pause once this week and tell one trusted adult what happened

### 8. Parent / Teacher Guide
- Avoid saying: "Anger is haram." / "Good Muslims don't get angry." / "Stop feeling that."
- Say instead: "Your anger is a signal. What is it telling you?" / "Let's do the pause together." / "The Prophet ﷺ taught us what to do in this exact moment."
- Interactive teach-back: put the four pause steps in order (same click-order activity as Lesson 1): Sit → Ta'awwudh + Breath → Silence → Water/Wudu
- One final scenario MCQ (same pattern as Lesson 1's Mariam question)
- Note: righteous anger (for injustice, for Allah's limits) is real and is not the enemy — teach children the difference between anger FOR myself and anger FOR what is right
